The problem

Twelve findings per knee, scored by macro AUC

The competition asks for the per-study probability of twelve clinically important findings on knee MRI: ACL and MCL injury, medial and lateral meniscus tears, osteoarthritis in three compartments, joint effusion, synovitis, Baker’s cyst, bone contusion, and fracture. Submissions run as offline Kaggle notebooks (no internet, ≤9 h) against ~1,300 hidden studies, scored by the average of the twelve ROC AUCs.

Each study is a real multi-site MRI exam: 5–6 series (sagittal / coronal / axial, fluid-sensitive fat-saturated or not), a median of 30 slices per series, mixed scanners and DICOM transfer syntaxes. The full training set is 265 GB across 819,640 DICOM files.

The twist

Almost no labels — but every study has a report

Only 58 of 4,407 training studies carry expert image-annotator labels. The other 4,349 come with just the original free-text radiology report — written in any of nine languages (English is only 39%; then Spanish, Turkish, Croatian, Greek, German, Bulgarian, Dutch, French). The test set has no reports at all.

That structure dictates the whole solution: text can only ever be a teacher. Any model that peeks at reports for its predictions cannot run on the test set. The competition is really asking: how well can you transfer knowledge from noisy multilingual text into a pure image model?

The solution

A two-stage teacher–student pipeline

Phase 1 — the teacher. An open LLM (Qwen2.5-14B + 7B via vLLM on one A100) reads every report and outputs a 0–100 confidence for each of the twelve findings. The 58 gold studies are never trained on; they are the only honest yardstick, used to validate and calibrate the teacher.

Phase 2 — the student. A study-level 2.5D network: a shared 2D backbone encodes 16 slices per series (3 adjacent slices as channels), attention pools slices into a series embedding, series metadata (plane, fluid-sensitivity) is injected, and a second attention pool produces the study embedding feeding 12 sigmoid outputs. Loss is BCE on the teacher’s soft labels, weighted per label by measured teacher reliability. One anatomy-specific detail: no horizontal flips in augmentation — they would swap the medial/lateral compartments that half the labels depend on.

Phase 1 in practice

Calibrating the teacher against 58 ground-truth studies

The first prompt scored a mean accuracy of 0.810 against the gold studies. Reading every disagreement revealed that the errors were not random — the image annotators used consistent thresholds the prompt didn’t know about:

  • Grade-1 sprains, interstitial signal, and mucoid degeneration are labeled absent; only fiber-discontinuity tears count.
  • “Small” or “trace” effusions are labeled absent (the naive prompt’s effusion specificity was 0.35).
  • Subchondral edema under cartilage lesions is not a contusion; edema around a fracture line is fracture, not contusion.
  • Chondromalacia counts as patellofemoral OA.

Encoding those thresholds into the rubric (v2), switching to soft confidences, and ensembling the two model sizes lifted teacher quality to mean AUC 0.876, with ACL at 0.98 and MCL at 0.97 — near the ceiling implied by the label noise itself, since a few gold labels contradict their own reports.

Phase 2 + Round 2

The student outgrows its teacher

Both backbones trained on soft teacher labels beat the teacher on the honest gold benchmark within ten epochs — the signature of successful distillation from noisy supervision. The clearest case is Effusion: the teacher manages only 0.86 (reports under- and over-mention fluid), yet the image model reaches 0.996 — fluid is simply visible.

Round 2 (noisy student): for each label, the training targets were re-blended between teacher scores and the model’s own out-of-fold predictions, with the blend weight chosen on the gold studies. The optimizer independently discovered the clinical structure of the problem — trust images for Effusion, Baker’s, OA, Contusion, Fracture; trust reports for ACL, MCL, Lateral OA. Retraining on blended targets lifted every fold: EfficientNetV2-S went from 0.87–0.88 to 0.89–0.90 gold AUC.

Negative results

Three experiments that said “stop”

Half the value of the sprint was establishing what doesn’t pay, each with a controlled run on the same folds:

Higher resolution (256 vs 224 crops): 0.880 vs 0.897 — losing crop augmentation cost more than the extra pixels bought.
2.5× model capacity (EfficientNetV2-M, 54M params): 0.885 vs 0.897 — capacity is not the binding constraint.
Bigger ensembles (12–16 checkpoints): 0.902–0.903 vs 0.907 for the best 4 — diluting the strongest family hurts.

Together these triangulate the real bottleneck: label quality, not model size, image resolution, or ensemble breadth. The strongest remaining lever is expert re-labeling of the weakest findings (Synovitis, PF OA), where a statistical relabeling attempt also failed honestly (leave-one-out AUC dropped from 0.76 to 0.70 — n=58 is too small to fit even two parameters).

Results

Leaderboard entries

  • Submission 10.915 public AUC — round-2 ensemble (ConvNeXt-T + EfficientNetV2-S, 8 checkpoints). Entered the board tied ~8th of 488 teams.
  • Submission 20.912 — efficiency-track variant: 4 checkpoints, parallel DICOM decode, ~2× faster. Within noise of the flagship at a fraction of the compute.
  • Submission 30.918 public AUC — current best. Round-3 ensemble (EfficientNetV2-S + SE-ResNeXt50, 8 checkpoints, test-time augmentation).

Local validation was honest throughout: the gold-58 estimate (0.90) slightly under-predicted the public score, and every modeling decision was made on out-of-fold or quarantined-gold data only — deliberate insurance against the public→private prevalence shift the organizers warn about.

Engineering notes

What it took under the hood

  • 265 GB in 20 minutes: the Kaggle CLI buffers whole downloads in RAM and dies on the HPC login node’s 8 GB cap — replaced with a signed-URL curl stream; single-threaded unzip (6 h projected) replaced with an 8-worker parallel extractor (35 min).
  • Reproducible fold discipline: language-stratified 4-fold CV (report language ≈ site proxy), gold-58 quarantined from all training, fixed seeds, every step committed to git.
  • Offline submission plumbing debugged early: Kaggle’s new input-mount layout, a P100 GPU pool incompatible with modern PyTorch (fixed by selecting T4), and bf16→fp16 fallback for pre-Ampere GPUs — all caught on the 3-study example set before any real submission was at stake.
  • Everything self-driving: downloads, environment builds, training waves and follow-on jobs ran as chained, monitored background processes across two machines — the human clicks were reserved for Kaggle’s UI-only actions.

Outlook

Where the next points are

With 10 weeks to the deadline: expert re-labels for the two label-limited findings (an hour of a musculoskeletal radiologist’s time on the pre-built review queue), longer training schedules and seed ensembles of the winning recipe, and per-label ensemble weighting fit on out-of-fold predictions. The efficiency track — where the 4-checkpoint variant already lives — is a second, less-contested prize pool that rewards exactly the distilled, fast models this pipeline produces.
