From 58 labels to 0.957: iterated noisy-student training on external knee MRI

The problem

Twelve findings per knee, almost no labels

The task: per-study probabilities for twelve knee findings (ACL, MCL, medial/lateral meniscus, osteoarthritis in three compartments, effusion, synovitis, Baker's cyst, contusion, fracture) on ~1,300 hidden multi-site MRI studies, scored by macro ROC AUC. Submissions run as offline Kaggle notebooks: no internet, ≤9 h on 2×T4.

Only 58 of 4,407 training studies carry human labels. Every other study has a free-text radiology report — in nine languages. The structural constraint that shaped everything: reports exist only at training time; the test set is images alone.

Method · 1

Reports as teacher

A two-model LLM ensemble (Qwen2.5-14B + 7B, vLLM) read all 4,349 unlabeled reports and emitted soft 0–100 confidences per finding, under a clinically calibrated rubric (grade-1 sprains and mucoid degeneration count as negative; trace effusion negative; edema around fracture is fracture, not contusion; chondromalacia counts as patellofemoral OA). Validated against the 58 gold studies: mean AUC 0.876 (ACL 0.98, MCL 0.97; weakest: synovitis 0.73). The 58 gold studies were quarantined — never trained on, used only as validation.

Method · 2

A study-level MIL student

One shared 2D backbone encodes 20 slice-triplets per series; attention pooling over slices gives a series embedding, which is concatenated with series metadata (plane, fluid sensitivity, fat suppression) and attention-pooled again over series into a single study vector with 12 sigmoid outputs. Recipe details that mattered:

  • Physical 140 mm center crop via DICOM PixelSpacing, decoded at 384 px — resolution without anatomy loss (a naive upscale was a confirmed negative).
  • Mirror augmentation with medial↔lateral label swap — plain horizontal flip would corrupt the four lateralized labels.
  • Per-label BCE weights proportional to teacher AUC; EMA 0.999 with one-epoch warmup.
  • Grouped CV folds by scanner ∪ byte-identical-report clusters (171 studies leaked across naive folds).

Method · 3 — the main lever

Iterated noisy student on 10,010 open fastMRI knees

The single biggest gain came from external unlabeled data: the NYU fastMRI knee DICOM collection (open access). Each round: pseudo-label every fastMRI study with the current best ensemble → pretrain a fresh trunk on pseudo-labels (4 epochs) → fine-tune on competition targets (8 epochs, warm-started, classifier head reset).

Round 1 alone was worth +0.02–0.03 per family. Iterating the teacher (re-pseudo-labeling with the improved ensemble) kept paying through round 3, with diminishing returns. ConvNeXtV2-Large — too big for our A100-40GB nodes — was unlocked with gradient checkpointing (timm.set_grad_checkpointing, batch 1 / accum 8), and became the best single family either teammate produced.

Every claimed gain was verified on the leaderboard, not just gold: teacher-quality 0.909 → LB 0.947; 0.9265 → 0.950; 0.9319 → 0.952. The gold→public mapping stayed linear for recipe changes — and broke the one time we used gold to select among near-tied ensembles.

Method · 4

Inference engineering for the 9-hour budget

  • Self-contained predict.py: in-memory DICOM decode (4-process pool forked before CUDA init), per-family crop sizes, capability-gated autocast (bf16 on Ampere, fp16 on T4).
  • Checkpoint sharding across both T4s (one thread per GPU; CUDA releases the GIL). This halved ensemble time and was the difference between the ConvNeXtV2-L combo timing out at 9 h and finishing with margin.
  • Fully automated submission chain: SLURM smoke test → dataset version upload → kernel push via kagglesdk (machine_shape="NvidiaTeslaT4") → save-run log verification → code submission via API.

Method · 5

Endgame: rank-blending onto the public stack

In the final ten days the public leaderboard was reshaped by shared notebooks (a public two-leg blend reached 0.956). Our models — trained on a pipeline nothing public resembled — were the ideal third leg: cross-correlation with the public stack was low where it mattered. A per-finding percentile-rank blend of the public Raptor CoAtNet, the public ConvNeXt-S6 folds, and our noisy-student pair, equal weights, with per-leg failure fallbacks, scored 0.957 public. Reweighting toward our leg tied at 0.957; adding a fourth, weaker, correlated public leg lost 0.002.

What the private leaderboard taught

The public meta collapsed; the method held

Final private scores dropped ~0.015 across the board — ours and everyone's. The public top of 0.966 resolved to a winning 0.960; our 0.957 blend landed at 0.942, outside the top 20. The rules said the public/private prevalence split differed, and the last-week public-notebook frenzy amplified exactly that overfit.

What transferred and what didn't:

  • Transferred: the noisy-student lane's relative gains. Our own-model submissions kept their ordering on private (0.935 > 0.932 > earlier), and the blend stayed our best submission on both boards.
  • Did not transfer: absolute public positions built on blending public predictions, for us and for the public-notebook pack alike.

Negative results

What we tried that did not work

  • Capacity: EfficientNetV2-M over -S, flat. Resolution without physical crop, negative.
  • Gold-57 ensemble selection: picking the max of 129 blend combinations on 57 studies overfit by ~0.002–0.003 (confirmed by a probe that scored below its gold-inferior sibling). Gold validates recipe changes; it must never select among near-ties.
  • Single-family submissions map ~0.005 below the multi-family line at equal gold — the LB prices architectural diversity that 57 studies cannot see.
  • Adding weak correlated legs to rank blends (the CoAtNet-224 experiment): −0.002.
  • Mirror-TTA for the MIL student, k=24 slices, 12–16-checkpoint uniform ensembles, statistical synovitis relabeling, per-label blend weights: all flat or negative.
